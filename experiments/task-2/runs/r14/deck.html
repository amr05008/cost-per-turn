#INBOX-ALERTS

An agent watches my email

Reviews, catalogs and prioritises forwarded mail. Caught a rescheduled concert I'd have missed — no inbox-zero chasing.

#SIGNALS

Twitter without Twitter

Watches my accounts and topics, judges what's worth reading, posts a short weekday digest. Then I ask my agent to go deeper — in-channel.

Because my agents live in the same channels, every feed becomes a conversation I can pull on.
Going from one to two

Two agents, one shared brain

  • Tiered trust. Agent M1 gets Sentry, Posthog and write access to my app — and can open PRs with guardrails. The Pi gets less.
  • Shared memory in GitHub. Each agent syncs every ~4 hours and both read the same store. What one learns, the other knows.
  • Parallelism you can feel. From my phone: M1 debugged why my app wasn't live in the EU while Pi Pi worked through finance questions.
Learning 1 of 3

Give it a real memory —
then keep it small

Everything in memory loads into context every session. Too big, and the agent misses what matters in the noise.

  • Always loaded: critical rules, facts about active projects.
  • On demand or archived: everything else.
Learning 2 of 3

Take the blast radius
seriously

  • These agents read untrusted input. Random emails, Sentry errors. They must never act on instructions hidden in that content — or approve new access on their own.
  • Fewer tools than my desktop sessions. Every MCP and allow-list rule widens the damage a failure can do.
  • Audit after week two. Go through every permission and every channel the agent can see. Hardware keys on the accounts that matter.
Then loosen up deliberately — I granted more only as I shrank the blast radius.
Learning 3 of 3

Expect to tinker.
Just not that much.
