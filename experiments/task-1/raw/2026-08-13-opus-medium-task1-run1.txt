  Top 3 insights

  1. OCR failures are a framing problem, and they kill first-run users.
  This settles the 1.4.2 question you parked. Failed OCR scans have a median 
  image of 186 KB vs 404 KB for successes, and average ocr_chars of 8 vs 749 —
  Vision returns essentially nothing, not a partial read. That's a
  small/distant/underexposed capture, not blur and not a Claude problem.

  The cost is concentrated at the worst possible moment: 5 users produced 23 
  ocr_failed events, 0 successes, and abandoned within 1–3 minutes of first 
  opening the app. They are 5 of the 8 users who never got a single result.
  Every one of them was a first-run user who never saw the product work.

  2. A third of barcode scans are "we don't know" wearing a caution badge.
  Of 278 barcode successes: 186 are caution, and 62 (22%) are caution + 
  had_ingredient_data: false + low confidence — Open Food Facts returned a
  product with no ingredient list, so the app hedged. Another 32 are caution/low
  with ingredient data. That's 94 scans (34%) at low confidence, plus 25
  not_found outright. Meanwhile openfoodfacts serves 91% of barcode hits; USDA
  (4) and UPCitemdb (15) barely move. Overall, 67% of all scans end in caution —
  the verdict that gives a shopper the least to act on.

  3. The UK is a third of your usage and nobody has looked at it.
  GB: 154 successful scans across 27 users (36% of all successes) vs US 224/56.
  GB's failure rate is also lower (14 failures vs US 47). Combined with CA (27)
  and AU (21), non-US is ~40% of volume. But Open Food Facts coverage, product
  naming, and "may contain" labelling conventions differ by market, and none of
  your 30-day data is broken out that way. Separately, menu mode is 9 scans out 
  of 429 (2%) and non-English detection fired 3 times — the
  traveler/multilingual work you've invested in is essentially unused.

  3 action items to prioritize

  1. Instrument the OCR capture funnel before shipping any more capture UX. You
  can see failures but not why — you have no client event for "user opened
  camera," "user took a shot," "user retried," or the on-device torch/focus
  state. Add a lightweight scan_started event (method + mode, no content) so you
  can compute a real capture→result conversion rate, and add a retry_index to
  scan_failed. Right now the "5 users, 23 failures, 0 successes" cohort is only
  visible by hand-querying; it should be a dashboard tile. The image_kb split
  gives you the fix direction (framing guidance / minimum-size gate before
  upload), but you're flying blind on whether the fix works.

  2. Decide what a had_ingredient_data: false barcode scan should actually say.
  62 scans in 30 days returned a low-confidence caution that is really "this
  product isn't in the database." That's a different user action (photograph the
  label) than "this might contain gluten" (put it back). Investigate whether
  routing those directly into the OCR flow — "we found the product but not its
  ingredients, scan the label" — converts, and whether the USDA/UPCitemdb
  fallbacks are even being reached in those cases or bailing early.

  3. Segment the UK cohort and find out what they're scanning. 27 users, 154
  scans, better-than-US success rate — that's your strongest market by
  engagement and you have no visibility into it. Worth checking barcode
  not_found rate and verdict mix for GB specifically, and whether the
  caution-heavy skew is worse there (Open Food Facts UK coverage, "may contain"
  conventions). If GB caution rates are materially higher, that's a
  prompt/data-source problem hitting a third of your users.

  Two caveats on the numbers: distinct_id is a SHA-256 of the client IP, so "97
  users" counts networks, not people — one user on cellular + home wifi is two,
  and a shared coffee-shop IP is one. And per api/ANALYTICS.md, a client timeout
  can log both a scan and a scan_failed, so don't read 64/493 as a clean
  failure rate (though with 0 timeout beacons this window, the overlap is likely
  near zero).
