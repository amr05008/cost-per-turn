⏺ Analyzed 30 days (2026-07-14 → 2026-08-13) via the query API. 430 scan + 64
  scan_failed across 89 distinct devices.

  Top 3 insights

  1. The modal answer is a non-answer — 71% caution, 33% low confidence.
  305/430 scans return caution, and 142 return confidence: low. That's not
  verdict conservatism spread evenly; it decomposes into two structural buckets
  that together are ~25% of all scans:
  - 62 barcode scans (22% of barcode) resolved a product but got no ingredient 
  list — 47 from Open Food Facts, 15 from UPCitemdb. Every one of them is
  caution/low. The waterfall found a name and nothing to analyze.
  - 47 OCR scans came back caution/low with ~419 chars of text vs ~990 chars for
  the high-confidence ones — same image size (384 KB vs 407 KB). The picture is
  fine; only part of the label made it into frame.

  2. Failures have collapsed since 1.4.1, and what's left is capture quality, 
  not connectivity.
  Weekly scan_failed: 29 → 16 → 7 → 1, while scan volume held ~95–104/wk.
  Connectivity failures (network) appear twice, both week of 07-27, none since —
  the 1.4.1 fix looks real. The remaining failure is lopsided by method: OCR 
  fails 19% of attempts (35/185); barcode only 8.5% (26/306). And hard OCR
  failures are a distinct mode from the low-confidence ones above — median 0 
  characters extracted, median image 186 KB vs 404 KB for successes. Tiny,
  detail-free images returning literally nothing.

  3. Small but real repeat usage, concentrated — and geographically not where 
  you'd assume.
  89 devices, 27 (30%) scanned once and never returned, but the top 5 devices
  drove 39% of all scans (75, 35, 21, 18, 18). The UK is 36% of scans (154) vs 
  US 45% (225), then CA 27 / AU 21. Web is effectively dead: 5 scans in 30 days
  across web + unknown platforms, and the unknown bucket fails more than it
  succeeds (12 failures vs ~2 successes in-window).

  3 action items to prioritize

  1. Build the "barcode found, no ingredients" hand-off. 22% of barcode scans
  dead-end in a caution/low verdict that tells the user nothing. Two things to
  investigate: whether the waterfall is actually reaching USDA/Nutritionix (only
  4 USDA hits in 30 days looks low against 47 OFF-without-ingredients), and
  whether the right UX is to immediately prompt "we found the product but not
  its ingredients — snap the label" instead of returning a verdict. This is the
  single largest addressable chunk of bad answers.

  2. Split the OCR capture problem in two before building 1.4.2 capture assist.
  The data says these are different bugs needing different fixes: hard failures
  = image too small / no detail (0 chars, 186 KB), low-confidence = well-exposed
  but partially framed (half the expected text). Framing guidance fixes the
  second; it won't fix the first. Worth adding a capture telemetry property
  (torch on/off, autofocus settled, crop-vs-full-frame) so the next release can
  tell which one moved. This confirms the earlier "framing, not blur" read —
  with the caveat that ~35 hard failures are a separate blur/distance problem.

  3. Add app version to the scan events. $lib_version is posthog-node 4.18.0 —
  the server SDK, not the app. There is no way today to attribute the
  failure-rate drop to 1.4.1 versus normal variance, or to measure whether 1.4.2
  capture assist works. Fix this before shipping 1.4.2, or you'll be reading
  the same ambiguous weekly chart afterward.

  Caveats: distinct_id is an IP hash, so "devices" over/under-counts real people
  (shared NAT, changing IPs) and the 75-scan device may be your own testing.
  The current week (08-10) is partial — 4 days. Per api/ANALYTICS.md, a client
  timeout can double-count as both scan and scan_failed; there are no timeout
  beacons in this window, so it doesn't bite here.
