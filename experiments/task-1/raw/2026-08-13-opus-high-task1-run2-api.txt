⏺ I pulled the full 30 days (Jul 14 – Aug 13) via the project's
  POSTHOG_PERSONAL_API_KEY against project 457245. Baseline: 494 scan attempts, 
  430 successes (87%), 89 distinct IDs. Backend is genuinely clean — 1
  claude_error in 494 attempts, zero server_error, zero rate_limited. So nothing
  here is an outage story; it's all product.

  Top 3 insights

  1. One in five barcode scans returns a verdict built on no ingredient data — 
  and users act on it anyway.

  62 of 280 barcode successes (22%) carry had_ingredient_data=false. Every
  single one maps deterministically to caution + low confidence. Add the 26
  not_found and 29% of barcode attempts produce nothing substantive.

  The behavioral half is what makes this the top finding. I looked at what the
  user does next:

  ┌───────────────────────────────────┬─────┬────────────────────────────┐
  │              Outcome              │  n  │ Retried with camera ≤5 min │
  ├───────────────────────────────────┼─────┼────────────────────────────┤
  │ not_found ("we couldn't find it") │ 26  │ 16 (62%)                   │
  ├───────────────────────────────────┼─────┼────────────────────────────┤
  │ Verdict, no ingredient data       │ 62  │ 15 (24%)                   │
  ├───────────────────────────────────┼─────┼────────────────────────────┤
  │ Verdict, real ingredients         │ 218 │ 31 (14%)                   │
  └───────────────────────────────────┴─────┴────────────────────────────┘

  When the app admits it failed, users fall back to the camera at 62%. When it
  hands back a content-free caution, retry sits at 24% — barely above the 14%
  baseline for a normal, fully-grounded scan. 47 of 62 times the user walked 
  away holding a gluten verdict derived from zero ingredient text. That's the
  app's least-safe output wearing the same UI as its best one. Source mix: 47
  Open Food Facts records with no ingredient field, 15 from UPCitemdb (which
  never returns ingredients at all).

  2. The OCR "failure rate" is mostly Apple, and the real failures are aiming, 
  not blur.

  Headline OCR failure looks like 36/186 (19%). But 14 of those come from
  Cupertino IPs and unknown-platform burst sessions — three tight clusters (Jul
  20, Jul 28, Aug 3; 3–6 attempts in under 3 minutes each) that are App Store
  review sessions. Every Apple review session hit 100% OCR failure and zero 
  successes. Strip those and real-user OCR runs 146 ok / 11 fail = 93%.

  This settles the open 1.4.2 question in
  plans/ocr-capture-assist-2026-07-18.md. image_kb vs. success, real iOS users
  only:

  ┌──────────┬─────┬──────┐
  │ image_kb │ ok  │ fail │
  ├──────────┼─────┼──────┤
  │ <200     │ 11  │ 0    │
  ├──────────┼─────┼──────┤
  │ 200–299  │ 17  │ 1    │
  ├──────────┼─────┼──────┤
  │ 300–399  │ 33  │ 4    │
  ├──────────┼─────┼──────┤
  │ 400+     │ 73  │ 5    │
  └──────────┴─────┴──────┘

  Small images never failed. Failures cluster in large, richly-detailed images 
  that yielded zero text — camera pointed at the front of the pack, not the
  ingredient panel. The Brooklyn user's Jul 20 session is the archetype: 873 KB
  → 428 KB → 405 KB, all zero chars, then 402 KB → 656 chars once they found the
  right side of the box. Confirms the earlier "framing guidance, not blur" read
  and kills the blur-gate idea.

  One safety edge case fell out: on 2026-07-19 a scan with 3 characters of
  extracted OCR text returned verdict safe. Low confidence, but "safe" is the
  word a celiac acts on. Everything else under 200 chars correctly degraded to
  caution.

  3. You cannot measure retention, and you can't attribute anything to a 
  release.

  distinct_id is a SHA-256 of the client IP, so a user on cellular becomes a new
  person every session. 80 of 97 IDs appear on exactly one day — that number is
  meaningless as churn. Meanwhile 3 IDs account for 31% of all events; the
  heaviest ([TOWN-GB], GB — 84 events over 12 days) went silent Aug 2 and there
  is no way to tell whether they churned or just changed networks. And because
  events ship server-side via posthog-node, $lib_version is 4.18.0 for all 494 —
  there is no app version on any event, so you can't tell whether 1.4.1's
  capture work changed anything.

  Two smaller things in the same data: GB is 34% of volume (168 events, 27 IDs)
  with AU and CA behind it — the international traffic is English-speaking, and
  detected_language fired 6 times in 30 days, so the ES/NL/CA/FR vocabulary work
  is not what your travelers need. And the web app is functionally dead: 3
  scans by 2 IDs in 30 days vs. 425 on iOS.

  3 action items to prioritize

  1. Fix identity and version stamping before investigating anything else.
  Generate a random install-scoped UUID on device (store it via
  mobile/services/storage.ts, per the CLAUDE.md rule), send it as distinct_id,
  and add app_version to every event. This is strictly more private than the
  current IP hash, so the privacy invariant is safe. Until it exists, retention,
  DAU, per-release comparison, and clean filtering of App Store review traffic
  are all guesswork — and every other item below is unmeasurable.

  2. Investigate the ingredient-less barcode verdict as a distinct product 
  state. The question to answer: should had_ingredient_data=false even render as
  a verdict? The retry data says users read it as one. Candidate to test:
  replace it with an explicit "found the product, but not its ingredients —
  photograph the label" state with a one-tap handoff into the OCR camera, and
  see whether the 24% retry rate moves toward the 62% that not_found already
  achieves. Also worth checking whether UPCitemdb (15 scans, never has
  ingredients) should be routed to this state unconditionally rather than
  through the verdict path.

  3. Rework OCR capture guidance around aiming, and floor the safe verdict. Two
  things from the same evidence. (a) The fix is on-screen framing help — a
  label-shaped guide and "point at the ingredients list, not the front of the
  pack" — not a blur/size gate, which the data shows would fire on images that
  succeed. Design it assuming a reviewer who points the camera at a desk, since
  that's exactly what Apple does and they have now had three consecutive
  all-failure sessions. (b) Add a floor so a sub-threshold ocr_chars extraction
  can never return safe; caution is the correct floor when there's nearly no
  text.

  Two things I'd explicitly deprioritize on this data: further multilingual
  vocabulary work (6 events in 30 days) and the web PWA (3 scans in 30 days).
