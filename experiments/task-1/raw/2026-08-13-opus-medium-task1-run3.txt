⏺ GlutenOrNot — 30 days (Jul 14 – Aug 13, 2026)

  Baseline: 429 successful scans, 64 failures, ~97 unique users. iOS is 424 of
  429 scans — web is effectively dead (3 scans, 3 users). US 52% / GB 36% /
  CA+AU 11%.

  Top 3 insights

  1. It's a one-and-done app: 82% of users never come back for a second day.
  80 of 97 users scanned on exactly one calendar day. Only 3 users account for
  151 events (35% of all volume). Worse, the first-run experience predicts it:
  of the 15 users whose very first scan failed, exactly 1 ever returned on
  another day (7%) versus 16 of 82 who succeeded first (20%). First-scan failure
  is roughly a 3× retention penalty on a tiny base — directionally strong, and
  it's the same cohort as the OCR problem below.

  2. Failed OCR scans are systematically low-detail images — this settles the 
  pending 1.4.2 question.
  Successful OCR scans have a median image_kb of 403 (p10 205); ocr_failed scans
  median 179 kb, p10 36 kb. And ocr_chars is 0.0 on every single failure —
  Google Vision returned literally nothing, not a partial read. That's not a
  "blurry text" signature (blur yields garbled partial text at normal file
  size); it's small/flat/low-texture frames — label too far away, badly cropped,
  or shot in poor light. Your earlier read of framing guidance, not blur is
  confirmed by the data. Encouragingly, ocr_failed fell 19 → 6 → 6 → 0 across
  the last four weeks (the last week is partial), so 1.4.1 already helped.

  3. A third of all verdicts are low-confidence "caution" — and 62 of them are 
  structurally uninformative.
  71% of scans return caution; 33% return low confidence. The barcode path
  explains the biggest chunk: 62 of 279 barcode scans (22%) came back with 
  had_ingredient_data: false, and 100% of those returned exactly caution/low —
  the app found the product but had nothing to reason over. Add 25 not_found
  failures and ~30% of barcode attempts end in "I can't tell you." Open Food
  Facts serves 93% of successful lookups; UPCitemdb (15 scans) never carries
  ingredients at all, so it only ever produces this dead-end.

  3 things to prioritize investigating

  1. Instrument the first session, then fix the OCR first-run.
  You can see that first-scan failures kill retention but not why users leave —
  there's no event between app open and scan. Add a lightweight capture-attempt
  event (frame/distance state, retry count) so you can distinguish "user gave
  up" from "user got a bad result." Ship framing guidance in 1.4.2 based on
  insight #2 — a minimum-detail check that blocks submission and says "move 
  closer to the label" before burning a Vision call, rather than after. Verify
  by re-running the image_kb split post-release.

  2. Decide what to do when the barcode path has no ingredients.
  22% of barcode scans return a verdict the app can't actually justify. Two
  options worth testing: (a) drop UPCitemdb from the waterfall entirely when it
  can't return ingredients and treat it as not_found, or (b) when ingredients
  are missing, explicitly hand off to the OCR path — "we found this product but 
  not its ingredients; snap the label." Right now that handoff is silent, which
  is likely a chunk of the one-and-done cohort. Also check whether the 25
  not_found barcodes cluster on UK products (36% of your traffic is GB, and Open
  Food Facts UK coverage is thinner than US).

  3. Kill or commit on web — and on the multilingual investment.
  Web produced 3 scans in 30 days against 424 on iOS; it's absorbing maintenance
  and cache-busting overhead for no usage. Separately, detected_language is
  non-null on 6 of 429 scans — the Spanish/Dutch/Catalan/French vocabulary and
  Traveler Context prompt work is essentially untouched by real traffic. Before
  extending either, work out whether that's genuinely no demand or an
  acquisition problem (all traffic is US/GB/CA/AU English-speaking, so you may
  simply have no distribution where those features matter).

  Caveat: per api/ANALYTICS.md, a client timeout can emit both scan and
  scan_failed for one attempt, so treat the ~13% failure rate as an upper bound.
  At these volumes (97 users) all retention splits are directional, not
  significant.
