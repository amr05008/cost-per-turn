  perfectly.

  clean gradient:

  The human cost is concentrated: five distinct IDs logged 23 OCR failures and 
  zero successes — ever. Each is a tight burst then silence (6 failures in 3
  min, 5 in 2.5 min, 3 in 43s with three identical 36 KB payloads). They
  downloaded the app, never got one working result, and left. Two payloads
  reached the endpoint at literally 0 KB.

  2. The app's most common answer is a non-answer, and it's a data-coverage 
  problem, not a model problem.

  71% of successful scans return caution; 33% return low confidence. On the
  barcode path, 62 of 279 scans (22%) came back with had_ingredient_data=false —
  and every single one was forced to caution/low. Add 26 not_found and ~29% of
  barcode attempts end with no real gluten answer despite the pipeline
  "succeeding." Open Food Facts carries 93% of hits; UPCitemdb (15 scans) never
  returns ingredients, so it's a structurally 100% low-confidence source; USDA
  fired 4 times; Nutritionix zero.

  3. A third of your traffic is now UK — and the two features built for 
  international users are unused.

  GB = 168 events (34%), from 15 distinct IDs across 4 cities in a single week —
  real adoption, not one traveler. AU 24, CA 27. Meanwhile menu mode is 9 scans
  out of 429 (2%), and detected_language fired 6 times total (sv, fr, it, en).
  The Spanish/Dutch/Catalan/French vocabulary work isn't being exercised; your
  actual growth is English-speaking. Backend health, separately, is clean: 1
  claude_error, 0 server_error, 0 rate_limited in 30 days.

  Top 3 to prioritize

  1. Find out why tiny images are being sent at all — then gate the OCR path 
  client-side.
  Before adding a user-facing "hold steadier" prompt, check whether mobile/
  capture code is compressing/downscaling to those sizes itself. If the client
  is manufacturing 36 KB and 0 KB payloads, this is a bug upstream of the user
  and the fix is free. Either way, add a pre-flight size check that re-prompts
  before the network call, and beacon a distinct scan_failed reason so it stops
  hiding inside ocr_failed. Highest leverage of anything here — it targets the
  zero-success cohort directly.

  2. Quantify the had_ingredient_data=false bucket and give it an exit ramp.
  Split those 62 scans: found in OFF with an empty ingredients field, vs. only
  resolvable via UPCitemdb. That split decides the fix — if it's mostly OFF
  gaps, the win is an in-app "no ingredient list for this product — photograph
  the label instead" hand-off that converts a dead end into an OCR scan. Also
  worth asking whether UPCitemdb earns its slot in the waterfall when it can
  only ever produce caution/low.

  3. Decide menu mode's fate before investing further.
  2% usage against real build cost. Investigate whether it's discovery (users
  don't know it exists — check where it sits in the UI) or demand (nobody wants
  it). That answer, plus the UK/AU/CA signal, suggests your next international
  investment is English-market polish, not more translation vocabulary.

  Two caveats on all of the above: distinct_id is a SHA-256 of client IP, so
  "users" is approximate in both directions (one person on cellular fragments
  into several); and per api/ANALYTICS.md, a client timeout can log one attempt
  as both scan and scan_failed, so treat failure rates as slightly optimistic.
