  This isn't only a review risk — it's the cold-start path. Of the 15 IDs whose

  ---
  Three things to prioritize

  1. Cut 1.4.2 with framing guidance — the data gate is cleared, and it says 
  skip blur detection.
  Don't add a client-side size gate; the naive all-traffic read would have
  talked you into one, and it would reject good captures while missing the real
  failures. The signal to act on is ocr_chars: 0 with a healthy image — users
  are photographing the wrong thing, or too far out. A reticle-aligned "fill the
  frame with the ingredients list" prompt on the retry screen is the fix. Ship
  it with the auto-retry-with-backoff already queued.

  2. Reproduce the App Review environment before 1.4.2 goes in. Run the OCR path
  in the Simulator against the stock photo library and confirm what happens. If
  picker-sourced stock images structurally can't OCR, ship a demo/sample-label
  affordance so a reviewer can see a green verdict on their first try.
  Highest-leverage item on the list: it's a live rejection risk on the next
  submission and it's the same first-run cliff costing you half of new users who
  start with a failure.

  3. Stop dead-ending the "found, but no ingredients" barcode case. 22% of
  successful barcode scans hit it, and the user gets an unexplained
  low-confidence caution. Instead: name the situation and hand off to the camera
  — "Found the product, but no ingredient list on file. Photograph the label?"
  That converts your single largest bucket of unhelpful answers into the OCR
  path, which succeeds ~92% of the time for real users. Worth splitting
  had_ingredient_data: false out in analytics so you can measure the conversion.
