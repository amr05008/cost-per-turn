⏺ Pulled 30 days (Jul 14 – Aug 13) from project 457245 via the query API.
  Baseline: 430 successful scans, 64 failures, 89 scanning users, 98.8% iOS.

  Top 3 insights

  1. OCR failure is a dead end, and image size predicts it almost perfectly

  Of 35 ocr_failed events, 33 were followed by another attempt within 10 minutes
  — but only 7 (20%) ever reached a success within 15 min. Users retry and fail
  again, then quit. Compare not_found (barcode): 19 of 26 recover. OCR failure
  is where the app loses people.

  The predictor is already in the payload:

  ┌──────────┬─────────┬────────┬──────────────┐
  │ image_kb │ success │ failed │ failure rate │
  ├──────────┼─────────┼────────┼──────────────┤
  │ <100     │ 3       │ 11     │ 79%          │
  ├──────────┼─────────┼────────┼──────────────┤
  │ 100–200  │ 10      │ 7      │ 41%          │
  ├──────────┼─────────┼────────┼──────────────┤
  │ 200–300  │ 18      │ 3      │ 14%          │
  ├──────────┼─────────┼────────┼──────────────┤
  │ 300–450  │ 57      │ 11     │ 16%          │
  ├──────────┼─────────┼────────┼──────────────┤
  │ 450+     │ 50      │ 3      │ 5.7%         │
  └──────────┴─────────┴────────┴──────────────┘

  Median failed image: 180 KB. Median successful: ~405 KB. One had image_kb: 0.
  The server learns this after the upload and round-trip; the phone could know
  it instantly.

  Encouraging: OCR failure rate by week ran 20% → 26% → 20% → 16% → 0/21 in the 
  partial Aug 9–13 week. That's consistent with the 1.4.x capture work landing,
  but 21 scans is not proof.

  2. The app's most common answer is "caution, low confidence" — and a fifth of 
  that is manufactured uncertainty

  Verdict mix: 71% caution, 21% safe, 8% unsafe. 35% of all scans are low
  confidence, up from 18% in week one and flat since.

  Two buckets are structural, not genuine risk:
  - 62 barcode scans (22% of the barcode path) had had_ingredient_data: false — 
  and 100% of them returned low-confidence caution. Product found, ingredients
  unknown, verdict indistinguishable from "contains malt extract."
  - OCR scans with <200 captured chars: 18 low / 1 medium / 0 high. Partial
  label capture — the user photographed part of an ingredient list and got a
  hedge.

  For a "can I eat this?" app, that's the core value proposition degrading into
  a shrug on roughly a third of scans.

  3. One-and-done usage, iOS-only, and a large unexplained UK share

  80 of ~96 users were active on exactly one day. Only 4 exceeded 3 days. There
  are real power users (top: 84 events over 12 days, [TOWN-GB] GB), but weekly
  actives are flat at 25–30 with no growth trend.

  Web is 3 of 430 scans — functionally dead as a surface. And GB is 34% of all 
  events from 27 users, second only to the US (272 events / 56 users) —
  disproportionate for a product whose barcode waterfall leans on USDA and whose
  fallbacks are US-centric.

  ⚠️ Caveat on all retention numbers: distinct_id is a SHA-256 of client IP, so
  a user who changes networks counts as a new user and a household counts as
  one. The 83% single-day figure is an upper bound on churn, and I wouldn't act
  on it directly (see action 3). The known scan/scan_failed overlap on client
  timeouts also means failure rates here are slightly understated.

  3 action items to prioritize

  1. Decide 1.4.2's capture scope with the image_kb data, then gate on-device.
  The image_kb split is the strongest lever in the dataset: a client-side
  pre-upload check that rejects sub-200 KB captures with a retake prompt would
  have prevented ~18 of 35 failures before a network round-trip, in the one
  place where users are demonstrably abandoning. Before building: re-run the OCR
  failure-rate-by-week query against the actual 1.4.1 rollout date. If the 0/21
  week holds up as the fix already working, 1.4.2 doesn't need this; if it's
  sample noise, this is the release.

  2. Split "we don't know" out of "caution."
  Investigate the 62 no-ingredient-data barcodes — specifically whether they
  skew GB, which would point at Open Food Facts coverage gaps rather than a
  generic long tail. Two things worth measuring first: do low-confidence
  cautions end sessions at a higher rate than confident verdicts, and what
  fraction of those 62 barcodes would a "photograph the label instead" fallback
  rescue? That determines whether the fix is a new verdict state, a data-source
  addition, or an in-app handoff to the OCR path.

  3. Fix the retention instrument before optimizing retention.
  Right now you cannot measure whether anyone comes back — IP-hash identity
  makes the single-day figure uninterpretable, and that blocks judging every
  other change. An install-scoped anonymous ID would fix it and doesn't touch
  the "no record of what you scanned" invariant, but it does change the privacy
  posture enough to warrant a policy/App Store label check before shipping.
  Worth doing early, because insights 1 and 2 both eventually need "did this
  bring users back?" to be answerable.
